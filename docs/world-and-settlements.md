# World and Settlements of Aetheln

## Document Status

This is the canonical world and settlement summary for the faction-based,
mandatory open-world-PvP direction. Settlement names remain subject to creative
and legal review.

Playable origins, anatomy, and cultural-heritage rules are defined in
[Playable Peoples](playable-peoples.md).

## Cosmology

Before the world there was the Deepwake, an ocean of unrealized dream. The
Waking Star emerged from it and dreamed Aetheln into physical existence.

The Star's light is life, magic, and memory. Three wounded Wardens tend its
remaining influence:

- **Sother:** kindling, dawn, law, and order.
- **Vael:** the deep, dusk, dreams, and mystery.
- **Ninh:** change, death, renewal, and turning.

The Wardens do not map cleanly to the two player factions.

## The Duskbreak

The Lucent Choir attempted to bind the Waking Star's light permanently into the
world and end death. Its ritual cracked the Star, glassed civilizations, and
began the world's slow dimming.

Fragments of the wounded Star now fall as Embers. Settlements need Embers to
survive, while the Ashen Choir gathers them to complete the original ritual.

## Present Conflict

The Concord traditionally regulates all contested Ember use. The world has now
divided between two coalitions:

- The **Dawn Concordat** believes shared law and central control are necessary
  for survival.
- The **Unbound Flame** believes local communities and finders must control the
  light they preserve or recover.

These are working faction names. Both factions fight the Ashen Choir while also
fighting each other over territory, Emberfalls, routes, ruins, and political
control.

## Territory Model

Every world region must declare:

- Territory owner or neutral status.
- PvP state.
- Recommended progression band.
- Respawn and reinforcement rules.
- Resource risk and banking locations.
- Faction objectives.
- Settlement services.
- Duskbreak scar.

Regions may contain more-specific subzones. The server resolves policy from the
region through the most-specific matching subzone; an explicit sanctuary,
respawn, beginner, or other protection rule takes precedence over a containing
mixed territory's hostility.

### Protected Starting Territories

Each faction has a distinct homeland experience. Rival players do not enter its
beginner districts during normal play.

These territories provide:

- Faction-specific opening quests.
- Class and combat training.
- Early equipment and permanent levels.
- Introduction to the faction Doctrine.
- A clear narrative reason to cross into mixed territory.

### Mixed Territories

Mixed territories host quests for both factions and enable mandatory faction
PvP except inside explicit server-authoritative sanctuary subzones. There is no
voluntary flag.

They combine:

- Shared PvE enemies.
- Faction-specific quest interpretations.
- Contested Emberfalls.
- Resource transport and banking.
- World bosses.
- Forts, routes, watchtowers, and field objectives.
- Safe mixed cities with sanctuary boundaries.

### Faction Capitals

Faction capitals support trade, training, governance, faction services, and
invasion gameplay.

Individuals may infiltrate through difficult routes. Organized groups may
attack gates and military objectives. Beginner districts remain inaccessible,
and invasion outcomes cannot permanently remove essential services.

Capital military and invasion subzones are separate security and topology
domains from protected beginner districts. An invasion route never grants
travel or simulation authority over beginner content.

## Major Settlements

### Kindlehold - The Last Lit City

Kindlehold surrounds the Kindlespire, the largest surviving shard of the Star's
light.

Revised role:

- Neutral seat of the Concord.
- Primary safe mixed city.
- Shared trade, crafting, diplomacy, and story services.
- Meeting point for cross-faction threats such as the Ashen Choir.
- Sanctuary where the server rejects hostile activation and effect application.

Kindlehold is not either faction's main capital. Its neutrality is unstable and
politically valuable.

### Branmark - The Tithe City

Branmark is an Aurin fortress city built over a failing light-well. Every lamp
is measured and every household contributes to the light-tithe.

Revised role:

- Major Dawn Concordat stronghold and candidate faction capital.
- Defensive Doctrine training and regulated Ember logistics.
- Invasion objectives centered on tithe gates, barracks, signal towers, and a
  protected Ember registry.
- Outer rings remain visibly glassed from the Duskbreak.

The final choice of faction capital remains open because all peoples may join
either faction.

### Graefell - The Undervault

Graefell is carved into the canyon where the first Kell awakened before the
Duskbreak. The catastrophe later turned the canyon to glass. Dying light is
preserved as memory in its geode archives.

Revised role:

- Politically aligned with Unbound arguments about local ownership.
- Crafting and memory-archive center.
- Possible Unbound Flame stronghold, but not automatically race-locked.
- Source of archive dungeons and disputes over whether memory can be privately
  held.

### Selaen - The Drift

Selaen is an airborne convoy of Vesh lantern-craft and dream-tech platforms
that follows predicted Emberfalls. Resonant lantern engines read and ride
predicted Ember currents to suspend and steer the convoy; Vesh bodies are not
aquatic and cannot fly.

Revised role:

- Mobile neutral or conditionally aligned settlement.
- Seasonal arrival point in mixed territory.
- Source of dream forecasts, rare trade, and changing world objectives.
- Safe sanctuary only while stationed inside a defined mixed-city boundary.

Its final political status remains open.

## Glasswake Reach

The Glasswake Reach is a plain of grassland and ruins fused into rippling glass
by the Duskbreak. Living pockets grow where new Embers seed the land.

It contains:

- The Weeping Spire.
- The Mirrorfields.
- Emberwatch.
- Lanternrest.
- The Duskvigil.
- The Hollow Choirhouse.

The existing PvE story follows refugees, unstable Ember-wells, the Ashen Choir,
and Choirmaster Sevrin Vale.

For production, the first Glasswake slice may remain a controlled cooperative
test region. Its final-world version must declare whether it is faction-owned or
mixed. That decision is still open and must be resolved before final quest and
server topology work.

### Emberwatch

A refugee waystation built around a cracked Ember-well.

Functions:

- Early quest services.
- Warden-priest presence.
- Repair, supplies, and travel.
- Block and defensive-combat teaching.

### Lanternrest

A Vesh caravan circle marked by a semi-living Mother-Lantern.

Functions:

- Dream-reading and Ashen Choir foreshadowing.
- Caravan and escort quests.
- Cosmetic and travel services.
- Dodge, pursuit, and threat-reading teaching.

### The Duskvigil

A Ninh-priest cloister overlooking the Hollow Choirhouse.

Functions:

- Dungeon staging.
- Ashen Choir investigation.
- Repair and travel.
- Interruption and encounter-preparation teaching.

## The Hollow Choir

The Hollow Choir is the initial cooperative dungeon. Sevrin Vale and the Ashen
Choir are attempting to complete the ritual that cracked the Star.

The dungeon teaches and tests:

- Telegraph recognition.
- Directional block.
- Dodge timing.
- Interruption.
- Guard pressure.
- Space control.
- Ally protection.

Its rewards feed permanent PvE progression and prepare characters for mixed
territory without making dungeon equipment an automatic PvP victory.

## Future Regions

### Faction Homeland A

The Dawn Concordat requires a complete starting region with its own settlement,
quest sequence, Doctrine introduction, and border transition. Branmark is a
candidate anchor.

### Faction Homeland B

The Unbound Flame requires an equivalent but not identical starting region.
Graefell is a candidate anchor.

Equivalent means comparable progression time, opportunity, and production
quality. It does not require mirrored geography or quests.

### The First City

The ruined golden-age capital was glassed almost whole. It is a future
high-level PvE, PvP, dungeon, or raid location.

### Grael Forest Holds

Future settlements built around beast-touched symbiosis.

### Emberborn Communes

Future communities of people reincarnated from spent Embers.

## World Flow

1. Faction-specific protected homeland.
2. Faction border and frontier warning.
3. Mandatory-PvP mixed territory outside explicit sanctuary subzones.
4. Safe mixed-city sanctuary for banking and services.
5. More dangerous mixed regions and world encounters.
6. Enemy infiltration and faction-capital invasions.
7. Seasonal movement of Selaen and major Emberfalls.

## Contested-Territory Objectives

Mixed territories should concentrate players around objectives with world and
faction value rather than make random killing the fastest progression path.

Objective types include:

- Capture and stabilize Ember-wells.
- Escort or intercept faction resource caravans.
- Protect or destroy siphon devices.
- Capture watchtowers, field camps, and forward respawn positions.
- Fight world bosses while competing with the enemy faction.
- Carry unstable resources to a controlled outpost before banking them.
- Defend quest locations and supply routes during faction events.

Territorial control may temporarily unlock travel, field services, resources,
or faction vendors. It must not create a permanent power snowball that prevents
the losing faction from recovering.

## Capital-Invasion Rules

Individual invaders may scout, sabotage limited military targets, hunt marked
military targets, and attempt small resource thefts.

Organized forces may breach gates, disable defensive structures, capture
staging points, contest an Ember store, and challenge a faction commander.

Confirmed limits:

- Beginner districts are inaccessible to invaders.
- Military/invasion subzones have no topology or authority path into beginner
  districts.
- Essential character services cannot be permanently disabled.
- Guards make unsupported attacks dangerous but do not replace defenders.
- Defenders receive a clear alert and a fast route to the threatened capital.
- Invasion results are temporary and recover after a defined cycle.
- A successful invasion cannot permanently transfer ownership of a faction
  capital.

Assault windows, target order, participant capacity, victory rules, reset time,
and reward values remain TBD.

## Mandatory-PvP Protection Rules

Protection comes from world structure and enforcement, not optional flagging.

- Rival players cannot enter faction starting territories.
- Safe mixed-city sanctuary policy rejects hostile ability activation and
  hostile effect application when the authoritative source or target is
  protected.
- Projectiles, persistent areas, periodic effects, summons, traps, forced
  movement, and other proxies pass the same server protection check at each
  gameplay application.
- Mixed-territory PvP cannot be disabled outside an explicit
  server-authoritative sanctuary or protected subzone.
- Respawn areas prevent immediate re-kills.
- Repeat-victim rewards rapidly decay toward zero.
- Killing a greatly lower-power player provides no normal kill reward.
- Suspicious reciprocal kills and kill trading provide no progression.
- High-risk repeat killers may receive visible bounties.
- Invasions cannot expose tutorial players or required beginner services.

## Faction Population Rules

Population imbalance must be managed without hidden damage or health bonuses.

- Measure active faction population by world, region, layer, and time window.
- Prefer contested layers with healthy opposing populations when capacity
  allows.
- Avoid unnecessary one-faction layers.
- Offer visible, capped resource or progression incentives to an
  underrepresented faction.
- Give defenders timely invasion travel and alerts.
- Prevent territorial rewards from creating permanent dominance.
- Preserve recovery routes after a sequence of losses.

Exact layer ratios, capacity rules, incentives, and faction-creation controls
remain TBD.
